## The model

**AI builds the shelves. The Trust writes the books, checks them, and keeps them.**

Each guide is assembled from Trust policy and national guidance, arrives roughly complete,
and flags contradictions between its sources. What it needs is a **quick expert eyeball**
from someone with knowledge and seniority in that area - a reading job, not a writing job.

Sign-off is per shelf, not per person-does-everything. A guide does not appear on the site
without its owner's sign-off; that gate is already built.

## Two things to settle before this list goes out

**1. Pilot scope, not all 68.** Lyndsay suggested it in the session (55:20) and Mike agreed:
let the two pilot wards say which guides they actually want. That turns 68 into a realistic
first tranche, gives the ward ownership, and tests the guide-building process on live demand
rather than on guesswork. The "In pilot?" column above is left blank deliberately - the wards
fill it in, not us.

**2. The 20 reds do not go to reviewers.** Either they are finished first or they are held
out of pilot scope. Sending a half-built guide to a safeguarding lead spends credibility that
is hard to earn back.
