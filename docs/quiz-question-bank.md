## Mental Health Act & Legal

**Q1. [Easy]** How long can a patient be detained for assessment under Section 2 of the Mental Health Act 1983?
- A. Up to 72 hours
- **B. Up to 28 days [correct]**
- C. Up to 6 months
- D. Up to 12 months
_Why:_ Section 2 permits detention for assessment for a maximum of 28 days, and cannot be extended or renewed.
_Source:_ Mental Health Act 1983 - Section 2 - https://www.mentalhealthlaw.co.uk/Section_2:_admission_for_assessment

**Q2. [Easy]** What is the maximum duration of the nurse's holding power under Section 5(4) of the Mental Health Act 1983?
- A. 2 hours
- **B. 6 hours [correct]**
- C. 24 hours
- D. 72 hours
_Why:_ Section 5(4) allows a suitably qualified nurse to detain an informal inpatient already receiving treatment for mental disorder for up to 6 hours, and it cannot be renewed.
_Source:_ Mental Health Act 1983 - Section 5 holding powers - https://www.mentalhealthlaw.co.uk/Section_5:_holding_powers

**Q3. [Easy]** What is the maximum duration of the doctor's holding power under Section 5(2) of the Mental Health Act 1983?
- A. 6 hours
- B. 24 hours
- **C. 72 hours [correct]**
- D. 28 days
_Why:_ Section 5(2) allows the doctor or approved clinician in charge (or their nominee) to detain a voluntary inpatient for up to 72 hours to allow a Mental Health Act assessment to be arranged.
_Source:_ Mental Health Act 1983 - Section 5 holding powers - https://www.mentalhealthlaw.co.uk/Section_5:_holding_powers

**Q4. [Easy]** How long does an initial detention under Section 3 (admission for treatment) of the Mental Health Act 1983 last before it must be renewed?
- A. 28 days
- B. 3 months
- **C. 6 months [correct]**
- D. 12 months
_Why:_ Section 3 lasts up to 6 months initially, may be renewed for a further 6 months, and thereafter renewed for periods of 12 months.
_Source:_ Mental Health Act 1983 - Section 3 - https://www.mentalhealthlaw.co.uk/Section_3:_admission_for_treatment

**Q5. [Easy]** Which professional makes the formal application to detain a patient under Section 2 or 3 of the Mental Health Act 1983 once the medical recommendations are in place?
- A. The Responsible Clinician
- **B. The Approved Mental Health Professional (AMHP) [correct]**
- C. The ward manager
- D. The nearest relative's solicitor
_Why:_ Doctors provide the medical recommendations, but it is the Approved Mental Health Professional (AMHP) who decides whether to make, and then makes, the application for detention (the nearest relative may alternatively apply).
_Source:_ Rethink Mental Illness - What is the Mental Health Act? - https://www.rethink.org/advice-and-information/rights-laws-and-criminal-justice/mental-health-laws/mental-health-act/

**Q6. [Easy]** Under Section 136 of the Mental Health Act 1983, the police may remove a person from a public place to a place of safety. For how long can that person normally be detained?
- A. Up to 6 hours
- **B. Up to 24 hours [correct]**
- C. Up to 72 hours
- D. Up to 28 days
_Why:_ Since the Policing and Crime Act 2017 changes, Section 136 detention lasts up to 24 hours, extendable by a further 12 hours only if assessment could not be completed for clinical reasons.
_Source:_ Mental Health Act 1983 - Section 136 (as amended 2017) - https://www.rethink.org/advice-and-information/rights-laws-and-criminal-justice/mental-health-laws/section-136-of-the-mental-health-act-emergency-police-powers-to-take-you-from-a-public-place-to-a-place-of-safety/

## Mental Capacity & DoLS

**Q21. [Easy]** How many statutory principles underpin the Mental Capacity Act 2005?
- A. Three
- B. Four
- **C. Five [correct]**
- D. Seven
_Why:_ The Mental Capacity Act 2005 is built on five statutory principles, including the presumption of capacity and that any act for a person lacking capacity must be in their best interests and least restrictive.
_Source:_ SCIE - Mental Capacity Act 2005 at a glance - https://www.scie.org.uk/mca/introduction/mental-capacity-act-2005-at-a-glance/

**Q22. [Easy]** Under the Mental Capacity Act 2005, a person must be assumed to have capacity unless it is established that they lack it. What is this principle called?
- A. The best interests principle
- **B. The presumption of capacity [correct]**
- C. The least restrictive principle
- D. The functional test
_Why:_ The first statutory principle of the Mental Capacity Act 2005 is the presumption of capacity: every adult has the right to make their own decisions and is assumed able to do so unless proved otherwise.
_Source:_ SCIE - Mental Capacity Act 2005 at a glance - https://www.scie.org.uk/mca/introduction/mental-capacity-act-2005-at-a-glance/

**Q23. [Medium]** As of 2026, which legal framework is in force in England for authorising a deprivation of liberty in a care home or hospital for someone who lacks capacity?
- A. The Liberty Protection Safeguards (LPS)
- **B. The Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) [correct]**
- C. Section 5(2) of the Mental Health Act
- D. There is no legal framework
_Why:_ The Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS) remain the operative framework. The Liberty Protection Safeguards (LPS), though passed in 2019, have not been implemented and remain delayed.
_Source:_ GOV.UK / CQC - Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards, 2025 - https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/deprivation-of-liberty-safeguards-england-2024-to-2025

**Q24. [Medium]** _Scenario: A patient who lacks capacity faces a decision about serious medical treatment. They have no family or friends who can be consulted about their best interests._ Which safeguard under the Mental Capacity Act 2005 must be instructed in this situation?

- **C. An Independent Mental Capacity Advocate (IMCA) [correct]**
- D. A nearest relative
_Why:_ An Independent Mental Capacity Advocate (IMCA) must be instructed for decisions about serious medical treatment or a change in accommodation when the person lacks capacity and has no appropriate family or friends to consult.
_Source:_ SCIE - Mental Capacity Act 2005 at a glance - https://www.scie.org.uk/mca/introduction/mental-capacity-act-2005-at-a-glance/

**Q25. [Hard]** Under the Mental Capacity Act 2005, the functional part of the capacity test asks whether the person can do four things. Which of the following is one of them?
- A. Name their next of kin
- **B. Retain the relevant information long enough to make the decision [correct]**
- C. Agree with the clinician's recommendation
- D. Demonstrate they have made a wise decision
_Why:_ The functional test requires the person to be able to understand, retain, and use or weigh the relevant information, and to communicate their decision. A person is not incapable merely for making an unwise decision.
